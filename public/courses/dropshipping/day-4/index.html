Foundations · Day 4 of 30

Choosing a niche you can win in

3.5 hours

Score several niche ideas and choose one with real demand, healthy margins and room for your own angle.

Lesson 30 minExample 20 minTasks 160 min≈ 3.5 h total

1Lesson

A niche is a group of people with a shared interest or problem: dog owners who groom at home, people who work from a desk all day, new parents, home bakers. Choosing a niche instead of selling random products helps you in three ways:

  • Your ads get cheaper because you speak to one kind of person.
  • Customers buy a second product from you, which raises profit per customer.
  • You build a brand that's worth more than a single product.

General store, niche store or one-product store?

Type Good for Watch out for
General store Testing many random products quickly Looks untrustworthy, hard to build a brand
Niche store Most beginners. Several related products for one audience Needs a clear audience from day one
One-product store A proven winner you want to brand properly Feels empty if the product fails

This course uses the niche store: you test three products for one audience, then turn the winner into a one-product page inside your niche store.

What makes a good niche

  1. Passionate or in pain. People spend on things they love (pets, hobbies) or problems that bother them (back pain, messy cables, sleep).
  2. Products sell for $25–$80. Enough margin for ads.
  3. Visual products. Things you can demonstrate in a 15-second video.
  4. Not dominated by one big brand. If everyone just buys from the market leader, you'll struggle.
  5. Repeat purchases or accessories. Refills, add-ons, a reason to come back.

How to score niches quickly

Use a 1–5 score for each:

  • Demand: Search the niche on TikTok and Instagram. Are there videos with 100k+ views? Are brands running ads (check Meta Ad Library)?
  • Margin: Can products sell for 2.5–3× their landed cost?
  • Passion/pain: Do people comment, ask questions and share?
  • Content: Can you easily film demos?
  • Repeat: Is there a second product they'd want?

Add the scores. Anything below 17 out of 25 is a pass.

Avoid these niches as a beginner

Electronics with batteries (returns, shipping restrictions), clothing (sizing returns), anything medical or supplement-based, and products owned by a big brand's trademark.
