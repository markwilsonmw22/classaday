"Complete it?": the half-heart text with a twist

Someone sends half a heart and asks you to complete it. The reply finishes the heart with what they really want. AI writes the punchlines.

ChatGPTClaudeGemini

Why it's trending

Blue Bear Creative's weekly social trends post for October 28, 2026 lists "Complete it?" at heat level 3 of 4: a half-heart text exchange where the reply completes the heart with what the sender actually wants.

How to use it

  1. Set up a short, friendly chat with a friend or family member who agreed to be in it. One person sends half a heart and "complete it?".
  2. Use the main prompt to brainstorm the twist: the reply that completes the heart with something unexpected, like a slice of pizza or the last cookie.
  3. Screen-record or screenshot the chat. Crop out real names, numbers and profile photos, or use nicknames.
  4. Post it as a photo, or a short video with the reply popping in on a beat.

Prompts

Main prompt: twist replies
There's a meme where someone texts half a heart and "complete it?", and the reply completes the heart with what they really want instead of love. Write 12 funny, clean versions of the reply. Each one is a short message plus the emoji or object that finishes the heart, for example "🍕 there, now it's complete". Make them about everyday things: snacks, sleep, the TV remote, the warm side of the bed, the dog. Keep each reply under 10 words. Nothing mean, rude or romantic-gone-wrong.
Variation: family edition
Write 8 versions of the "complete it?" half-heart text meme where the chat is between family members. The parent or sibling completes the heart with a chore, a reminder or a household thing, like "🧺 the laundry is still in the dryer". Make them warm and funny, the kind of thing a mom or brother would actually send. Each reply under 12 words.
Variation: pet's point of view
Write 8 versions of the "complete it?" half-heart text meme where the reply comes from a pet, as if the pet could text. The pet completes the heart with what it really wants: treats, a walk, the sunny spot on the couch, the box the parcel came in. Use a simple emoji to finish the heart and keep each reply under 10 words.
Variation: small business
I run a [type of business, like a coffee shop, bakery or bike shop]. Write 8 versions of the "complete it?" half-heart text meme for our social media. A customer sends half a heart and "complete it?", and our account replies by completing it with something we really sell or do: [list 3 to 5 real items or services]. Keep it playful and under 12 words per reply. No prices, no discounts, no made-up claims and no fake customer names.

Tips

  • Only use chats with people who agreed to be in the post, and hide real names, phone numbers and photos.
  • The surprise is the whole joke. Keep the first message sweet so the twist lands.
  • For a business, make it clear it's a playful post from your own account, not a real customer conversation.
  • Post a few versions as a carousel and ask followers which one is most like them.

Results vary between tools and runs. Make a few versions and keep the best. Only use photos of yourself, or of people who have agreed, and label AI content where the platform asks.
