Not all angels are in heaven…

"Not all angels are in heaven… some are" plus your whole personality. AI helps you find a funny, true ending that fits your job or your quirks.

ChatGPTGeminiClaude

Why it's trending

An Instagram Reels trends roundup updated October 23, 2026 lists "Not all angels are in heaven" as a trend from October 16: on-screen text starts the line, and creators finish it with something that fits their job or their quirks.

How to use it

  1. Film a short clip of yourself doing the thing that makes you you: at your desk, in your kitchen, behind the counter.
  2. Use the main prompt to get a list of endings, then pick the one that makes you laugh.
  3. Add "Not all angels are in heaven…" as text first, then reveal the ending a second later.
  4. Post with a trending sound from the app.

Prompts

Main prompt
Help me finish this on-screen text: "Not all angels are in heaven… some are". Give me 10 endings, each under 12 words. Make them about me, based on these details: my job is [your job], my funny habits are [two or three habits], and people always ask me for help with [one thing]. Keep it kind and a little self-mocking. No hashtags, no emojis.
Variation: the friend group
Give me 8 endings for "Not all angels are in heaven… some are" about a friend group role, like the one who always drives, the one who brings snacks or the one who remembers every birthday. I'm usually [your role]. Each ending under 12 words, warm and funny.
Variation: small business
I run [your type of business]. Write 8 endings for "Not all angels are in heaven… some are" from the point of view of my business or my team. Think small, real things we do for customers, like [one or two examples]. Each ending under 12 words. Friendly, not salesy. Don't make up prices, reviews or deadlines.

Tips

  • The more specific the ending, the better. "Answering emails at 11 p.m." beats "working hard".
  • Leave a short pause before the ending so people read the first part.
  • Keep the clip quiet and simple so the text does the work.
  • Only use friends in the video if they've said yes.

Results vary between tools and runs. Make a few versions and keep the best. Only use photos of yourself, or of people who have agreed, and label AI content where the platform asks.
