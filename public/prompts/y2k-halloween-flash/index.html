Y2K Halloween flash photo

Turn a selfie into a candid early-2000s Halloween snapshot: harsh flash, film grain and a date stamp.

ChatGPTGemini

Why it's trending

Throwback Halloween photos that look like they were found in a drawer are one of this October's most shared AI looks. The harsh on-camera flash is what sells it.

How to use it

  1. Pick a clear, front-facing selfie in even light.
  2. Attach it to ChatGPT or Gemini and paste the main prompt.
  3. Ask for two or three versions and keep the one where your face looks most like you.

Prompts

Main prompt
Use my attached photo. Keep my face, hair and features exactly as they are. Recreate it as a candid photo from around 2003: a teenager's bedroom at night, a small boxy TV glowing in the background, orange string lights, posters slightly out of focus. I'm wearing a plain black top, holding plastic vampire fangs and half-laughing. Harsh direct on-camera flash like a cheap disposable camera, deep shadows behind me, slight red-eye, visible film grain, warm faded colours, and a small orange date stamp in the bottom-right corner that reads 10 31 03. Vertical 4:5.
Variation: cat ears
Same photo and same 2003 flash look, but I'm wearing black cat ears and have whiskers drawn on in eyeliner, sitting on the edge of a bed with a bowl of candy. Keep my face exactly the same. Film grain, harsh flash, 4:5.
Variation: friends
Use the attached photo of my friends (everyone in it has agreed). Keep every face exactly the same. Make it a 2003 house-party Halloween snapshot: homemade costumes, a kitchen with fridge magnets, red plastic cups, harsh disposable-camera flash, motion blur on one person laughing, film grain, date stamp 10 31 03. 4:5.

Tips

  • If your face changes, add: "Do not change my face shape, eyes, nose or skin tone."
  • Keep the date stamp short. Long text often comes out garbled.
  • Post it as a carousel with your real photo second. People love the before and after.

Results vary between tools and runs. Make a few versions and keep the best. Only use photos of yourself, or of people who have agreed, and label AI content where the platform asks.
