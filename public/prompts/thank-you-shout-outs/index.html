Thank you, baby: shout-outs for the small things

A quick, slightly sappy thank-you list for the people and little things that make life better. AI helps you turn vague thanks into short, specific lines.

ChatGPTGeminiClaude

Why it's trending

A weekly social media trends roundup published October 28, 2026 lists "Thank You Baby" in its October 21 section: creators use a catchy thank-you audio to spotlight things they're grateful for, like a partner who does the dishes or a friend with great food tips.

How to use it

  1. Jot down a few people and small things you're grateful for this week.
  2. Use the main prompt to turn them into short, specific thank-you lines.
  3. Film a quick clip for each line, or one clip with the lines appearing in turn, and add the trending thank-you sound from the app.
  4. Tag the people you thanked, if they're happy to be in it.

Prompts

Main prompt
Help me make a short gratitude video. Turn this list into 6 thank-you lines for on-screen text, each under 10 words, each starting with "Thank you". Make them specific and a little sappy, not generic. Here's what I'm grateful for: [your list, for example: my partner doing the dishes, my friend who always picks the restaurant, my dog waiting at the door]. No hashtags, no emojis.
Variation: funny version
Write 8 short, funny thank-you lines for on-screen text, each under 10 words, thanking small everyday things as if they were heroes. For example the last clean mug, a green light when I'm late, or my phone at 1 per cent hanging on. Keep it warm and silly.
Variation: a note to send
I want to send a short thank-you message to [who] for [what they did]. Write 3 versions under 50 words each: one heartfelt, one funny, one that fits in a text. Mention the specific thing they did and how it helped me. Plain words, no clichés.
Variation: small business
I run [your type of business] in [your town]. Write 8 thank-you lines for a short video, each under 10 words, where our team thanks real groups of people: regulars, first-time visitors, our suppliers, our staff, people who share our posts. Mention real things we do, like [one or two examples]. Warm, not salesy. Don't make up customer names, numbers, reviews or deals.

Tips

  • Specific beats general. "Thank you for always saving me the window seat" hits harder than "thanks for everything".
  • Ask before you tag or film someone you're thanking.
  • Keep each line on screen long enough to read: about two seconds.
  • Businesses: thank groups, not invented customers. Never make up quotes or numbers.

Results vary between tools and runs. Make a few versions and keep the best. Only use photos of yourself, or of people who have agreed, and label AI content where the platform asks.
