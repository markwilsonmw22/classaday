Product research · Day 5 of 30

What makes a winning product

3.5 hours

Learn the checklist that filters out products that will lose money, so research time goes only to products with a chance.

Lesson 25 minExample 15 minTasks 170 min≈ 3.5 h total

1Lesson

A "winning product" isn't magic. It's a product that passes a short list of tests, so that ads can sell it at a profit. Use this checklist on every idea from now on.

The 10-point checklist

  1. Solves a problem or creates a "wow". Either fixes something annoying (shedding hair everywhere) or makes people say "I need that" when they see it work.
  2. Easy to demonstrate in seconds. If you need a paragraph to explain it, a scrolling viewer won't get it.
  3. Not easy to find in local stores. If it's on the shelf at Walmart or Canadian Tire for $12, few people will pay you $35.
  4. Sells for $25–$80. Below $25 the ad maths rarely works (Day 2). Above $80, buyers want reviews and faster shipping than most suppliers offer.
  5. 2.5–3× markup or better on landed cost.
  6. Small and light. Cheaper to ship, fewer damages.
  7. Not fragile, not sized. Glass breaks. Clothes and shoes come back.
  8. No trademark or brand issues. No logos, character designs or patented look-alikes.
  9. Allowed on ad platforms. No weapons, adult items, supplements, medical claims or "before/after" body content.
  10. Has an angle. A specific person and moment you can show: "for golden retriever owners in shedding season."

A product needs to pass at least 8 of 10, and must pass 4, 5, 8 and 9 without exception.

Demand signals that mean something

Likes alone mean little. Look for:

  • Comments asking "where can I buy this?" or "link?"
  • Several different stores running ads for the same product. One store could be luck; five means it sells.
  • Ads that have been running for 30+ days. Nobody keeps paying for an ad that loses money. Meta Ad Library shows when each ad started.
  • Rising search interest on Google Trends over the last 12 months.
  • Real order counts on AliExpress or CJ, with recent reviews and photos.

Saturation: how worried should you be?

A product that many stores sell can still work for you if you bring a new angle, a better offer or better creative. What kills you is selling the same product with the same video at the same price as twenty others. Early in your research, saturation is a small concern. During teardown (Day 8) you'll check whether you can stand out.
