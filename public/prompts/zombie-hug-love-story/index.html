Zombie hug: the last embrace

A short, sad-sweet clip: your partner or best friend steps out as a zombie, you recognize them, and you hug anyway. Then it cuts to a warm memory.

SeedanceHiggsfieldKling

Why it's trending

Trend guides updated October 15 to 19, 2026 report a "zombie love story" or "zombie hug" clip that spread from Instagram in late September to TikTok in early October under #zombieaitrend, with Google searches for "zombie trend" peaking on October 4.

How to use it

  1. Pick two clear, front-facing photos: one of you and one of your partner, friend or family member who has said yes.
  2. Upload both to an image-to-video tool that takes two reference photos and paste the main prompt. Ask for 10 to 15 seconds, vertical 9:16.
  3. If faces drift, make the two scenes as separate clips, then join them in your video editor with a quick cut.
  4. Post it with a soft, slow piano sound and a short caption like "I'd still pick you".

Prompts

Main prompt
Use the two people in my photos. Scene 1: a quiet snowed-in cabin at dusk, cold blue-grey light, snow drifting past the window. Person A stands by the door, nervous and teary. The door creaks open and Person B steps in as a gentle movie-style zombie: pale grey skin, cloudy eyes, messy hair, the same clothes, still clearly the same face. Person A freezes, then recognizes them, softens and opens their arms. Person B stumbles forward into a long, tight hug. Hard cut at the hug. Scene 2: the same two people laughing together in a golden wheat field at sunset, warm and bright. Keep both faces exactly as in the photos. No weapons, no blood. Cinematic, slow camera push-in. Vertical 9:16, 12 seconds.
Variation: best friends on a rooftop
Use the two people in my photos. Scene 1: a rainy city rooftop at night, neon glow on wet concrete. Person A waits under an umbrella. Person B shuffles out of the stairwell door as a soft, not-scary zombie with pale skin and tired, cloudy eyes, wearing the same hoodie as in the photo. Person A drops the umbrella, smiles through tears and pulls them into a hug while the rain keeps falling. Hard cut. Scene 2: the same two friends laughing and sharing fries on a sunny beach boardwalk. Keep both faces exactly as in the photos. No gore. Vertical 9:16, 10 seconds.
Variation: your pet
Use the person and the pet in my photos. Scene 1: a dim farmhouse kitchen at night, moonlight through the window. The person sits on the floor, worried. The pet pads in as a cute, cartoon-like zombie version of itself: grey-green fur, droopy ears, sleepy glowing eyes, same markings and collar. The person laughs, opens their arms, and the pet jumps into their lap for a cuddle. Hard cut. Scene 2: the same person and pet running across a sunny backyard in autumn leaves. Keep the pet's face and markings exactly as in the photo. Nothing scary. Vertical 9:16, 10 seconds.
Variation: small business
Use the person in my photo as the shop owner. Scene 1: a cozy small shop at closing time on Halloween night, string lights on, pumpkins on the counter. The owner hears the door bell. A friendly zombie customer shuffles in: pale skin, cloudy eyes, torn cardigan, holding an empty mug. The owner smiles, fills the mug, and the zombie gives them a big grateful hug over the counter. Hard cut. Scene 2: the same shop the next morning, bright and busy, with a chalkboard sign that reads "[your shop name]: even zombies come back". Keep the owner's face exactly as in the photo. No gore. Vertical 9:16, 12 seconds.

Tips

  • Only use photos of people who have agreed to be in the video, and show them before you post.
  • Keep it tender, not gory. Leave out weapons and blood; the hug is the whole point.
  • The cut to the warm memory works best right as the hug starts, around 8 to 10 seconds in.
  • If text on the chalkboard comes out wrong, leave it out and add your shop name as an on-screen caption.

Results vary between tools and runs. Make a few versions and keep the best. Only use photos of yourself, or of people who have agreed, and label AI content where the platform asks.
