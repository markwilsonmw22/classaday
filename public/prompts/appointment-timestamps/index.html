Appointment timestamps: one visit, walk-in to walk-out

A timestamp vlog of just one appointment, from the moment you walk in to the moment you leave. AI turns your notes into short timed captions.

ChatGPTGeminiClaude

Why it's trending

An Instagram Reels trends roundup updated November 13, 2026 lists "Appointment timestamps" as a new format that week. It's a twist on the timestamp vlog (see our October pack "Vlog timestamps"): instead of a whole day, it covers one appointment, walk-in to walk-out.

How to use it

  1. Pick an everyday appointment: a haircut, nails, a car service, a tattoo touch-up, a pet groom. Ask the business first if you can film.
  2. Film a 2–3 second clip at each moment and note the time.
  3. Paste your notes into the main prompt to get one timed caption per clip.
  4. Add the time and caption to each clip, use quick cuts, and end on the finished result.

Prompts

Main prompt
Turn my notes into on-screen captions for an appointment timestamp video, from walk-in to walk-out. The appointment: [e.g. a haircut]. My notes: [paste times and what happened, e.g. 2:58 p.m. walk in, 3:05 the big decision, 3:40 first look in the mirror]. Each caption starts with my real time, then one line under 10 words. Make it honest and a bit funny, like I'm telling a friend. End with a short line for the final reveal. No hashtags, no emojis.
Variation: winter tire swap
Write appointment timestamp captions for getting my winter tires put on in Canada. My real times and moments: [list them, e.g. 8:00 a.m. drop off, 8:20 coffee in the waiting room, 9:15 keys back]. Each line starts with the time and is under 10 words. Warm and a little funny about the yearly tire rush. Close with a short line about feeling ready for snow.
Variation: small business
I run [type of business, e.g. a salon or pet grooming shop]. Write timestamp captions for one client appointment, walk-in to walk-out, filmed with the client's permission. Real moments: [list times and steps]. Each line starts with the time and is under 10 words. Show the care and skill behind each step. Friendly, not salesy. Don't make up reviews, quotes, prices or results.

Tips

  • Use your real times. The fun is seeing how long each step actually took.
  • Always ask the business and anyone else in the shot before you film.
  • Save the best clip, the finished look, for the very end.
  • Keep it under 30 seconds. Cut any step that doesn't change much on screen.

Results vary between tools and runs. Make a few versions and keep the best. Only use photos of yourself, or of people who have agreed, and label AI content where the platform asks.
