Web scraping & data · Day 14 of 30

Lead lists, enrichment and outreach rules

3.5 hours

Build a clean, scored list of potential clients from legitimate sources, find the right contact, and learn the anti-spam rules before you send a single email.

Lesson 35 minExample 20 minTasks 155 min≈ 3.5 h total

1Lesson

Every agency needs a steady flow of potential clients. Today you'll build a prospect list the right way: from public business information, scored by how much each business needs your help, and ready for compliant outreach.

Where to find businesses

Source How Notes
Google Maps (by hand) Search "[niche] [city]" and record businesses Slow but free and allowed
Google Places API Official API that returns business names, addresses, websites, ratings Paid per request with a free allowance; Google's terms limit how long you may store the data, so read them
Industry directories HomeStars, chamber of commerce member lists, association directories Check each site's terms before scraping; many allow manual use only
Business registries and local lists City business directories, BIA member pages Often public and up to date
Your network Friends, family, previous employers, other freelancers Warmest leads you'll ever have
LinkedIn Search and Sales Navigator, by hand LinkedIn's user agreement forbids scraping; research manually

A quick example of the official Places API (New) Text Search in Python (you'll need an API key from Google Cloud):

import requests

API_KEY = "YOUR_API_KEY"
response = requests.post(
    "https://places.googleapis.com/v1/places:searchText",
    headers={
        "Content-Type": "application/json",
        "X-Goog-Api-Key": API_KEY,
        "X-Goog-FieldMask": "places.displayName,places.formattedAddress,"
                            "places.websiteUri,places.rating,places.userRatingCount",
    },
    json={"textQuery": "plumber in London, Ontario"},
    timeout=15,
)
for place in response.json().get("places", []):
    print(place["displayName"]["text"], "|", place.get("websiteUri", "no website"),
          "|", place.get("rating"), f"({place.get('userRatingCount', 0)} reviews)")

Score every lead

You're looking for businesses that can afford you and clearly need you. Score each 0–2:

Signal 2 points 0 points
Website Slow, not mobile-friendly or missing Modern and fast
Google Business Profile Few photos, no posts, unanswered reviews Fully optimised
Reviews Fewer than top competitors Far more than competitors
Ads Running ads (they spend on marketing) No sign of marketing spend
Size 3–20 staff, busy, established One-person side business

Check ads in the Meta Ad Library and the Google Ads Transparency Center (both free and public). Contact the highest scores first.

Finding the right contact

  • The business's own website: About, Team and Contact pages.
  • Hunter.io (domain search) finds publicly published business email addresses and patterns; Apollo and similar tools add company data. Verify emails before sending (Hunter's verifier, NeverBounce or ZeroBounce) to protect your sender reputation.
  • Prefer a named owner or manager over info@.

The rules: Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL)

CASL applies to any commercial electronic message (email, text, DM) sent to or from Canada. It's strict, and penalties can reach $1 million per violation for individuals and $10 million for businesses. This is general information, not legal advice; read the CRTC's guidance.

You need consent before sending. For cold outreach to businesses, the key route is implied consent through conspicuous publication. It applies only when all of these are true:

  1. The person's email address is published conspicuously (for example, on the business's website or a directory),
  2. It's not accompanied by a statement that they don't want unsolicited commercial messages, and
  3. Your message is relevant to their business role (marketing services pitched to the owner or marketing manager: yes; a random product: no).

Every message must also:

  • Identify you: your name, business name, and a mailing address plus a phone number, email or web address.
  • Include an unsubscribe that works, and remove anyone who unsubscribes within 10 business days.

Keep records: a screenshot or note showing where and when you found each address. The burden of proving consent is on the sender.

In the US, CAN-SPAM is less strict (opt-out based) but also requires honest subject lines, a physical address and a working unsubscribe.

Bring it into your CRM

Import the scored list into HubSpot Free CRM with columns: business, contact, role, email, phone, website, source of email, date found, score, notes. You'll use it on Day 27.

2Worked example

Riya's first 60-lead list.

She searches "plumber", "HVAC", "electrician" and "roofing" in London and St. Thomas by hand on Google Maps and records 140 businesses. She removes national chains and one-person operations: 82 left.

Scoring takes an evening. 23 businesses score 7 or more: slow sites, few reviews compared with competitors, and 9 of them are running Facebook ads that send people to their homepage.

For each of the 23, she finds the owner's business email on the company website, screenshots the contact page (her CASL record), verifies the email, and imports everything into HubSpot. One owner's site says "no solicitations": she removes him from the email list and adds him to a "local events" list to meet in person instead.

✓By tonight you'll have

★Tip

Quality beats volume. Ten well-researched, personal emails to high-scoring businesses will win more clients than 1,000 generic ones, and they keep you on the right side of the law.
