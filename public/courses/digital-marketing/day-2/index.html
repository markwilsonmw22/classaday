Foundations & your agency · Day 2 of 30

Marketing fundamentals: customer, positioning and offer

3.5 hours

Learn the three things every campaign depends on (who it's for, why them, and what they get) and write them for a real business.

Lesson 35 minExample 25 minTasks 150 min≈ 3.5 h total

1Lesson

Here's a secret most agencies learn the hard way: you can't out-post a bad offer. Before any SEO or Reels, every client needs three things clear: the customer, the positioning and the offer. Get these right and every channel works better.

1. The ideal customer profile (ICP)

An ICP describes the people who are most valuable to the business and easiest to win. Not "everyone". Be specific:

  • Who: age range, life stage, job, location
  • Problem: what's annoying, painful or missing for them right now
  • Trigger: what moment makes them search or buy (a burst pipe, a new baby, a first apartment)
  • Objections: what makes them hesitate (price, trust, time)
  • Where they hang out: platforms, groups, places, publications
  • Words they use: phrases from reviews, comments and calls

The best source of these words is real customers. Read Google reviews (theirs and competitors'), Reddit threads, Facebook group posts and support emails. Copy the exact phrases.

2. Jobs to be done

People don't buy a product, they "hire" it for a job. Nobody wants a drill, they want a shelf on the wall. Ask: what job is the customer hiring this business to do?

  • The café's job: "Give me a calm place to work for two hours that feels like a treat."
  • The plumber's job: "Make the emergency go away today, without being ripped off."

Once you know the job, the marketing writes itself. The café should sell calm and Wi-Fi, not "artisanal beans". The plumber should sell speed and honesty, not "quality plumbing services".

3. Positioning

Positioning is the answer to "why you instead of the alternatives?" Fill in this sentence:

For [customer] who [need or trigger], [business] is the [category] that [key benefit], unlike [alternative], because [proof].

4. The offer

An offer is everything the customer gets for what they pay, and the reasons to act now. A strong offer has:

Part Café example Plumber example
Core product Coffee, food, workspace Repair or installation
Bonus Free refill for laptop workers before 11am Free camera inspection with any drain job
Guarantee "If you don't love it, we'll remake it" "Fixed price before we start. No surprises."
Urgency (honest) Seasonal drink, limited time Same-day slots, first come first served
Easy next step Order ahead on the app One-tap call button, online booking

Never fake urgency or scarcity. Real limits (seasonal menus, booking slots) work just as well and keep trust.

5. Brand voice

Three words that describe how the business talks, plus three it never sounds like. Corner Grind: warm, witty, local; never corporate, preachy, try-hard. Write them down; you'll use them for every caption.

2Worked example

Riya's discovery session with Northfield Plumbing.

She reads 120 Google reviews of Northfield and its three biggest competitors and copies phrases into a sheet. Patterns jump out:

  • Good reviews say: "showed up when they said", "explained everything", "price was what they quoted".
  • Bad competitor reviews say: "never called back", "bill doubled", "tracked mud through the house".

Her positioning statement:

For London homeowners who have a plumbing problem they need fixed today, Northfield Plumbing is the local plumber that gives a fixed price before starting and shows up on time, unlike big franchise chains, because every job is quoted upfront and every tech wears shoe covers. (412 five-star reviews.)

The owner reads it and says, "That's exactly us. We've just never said it." Every ad, page and post in the coming weeks will repeat that promise.

3Today's tasks

✓By tonight you'll have

An ICP, a voice-of-customer sheet, a positioning statement and an offer for one real business.

★Tip

If you can't fill in the "because [proof]" part of the positioning, the business has a product problem, not a marketing problem. Tell them kindly. It's the most valuable thing you'll say all week.
