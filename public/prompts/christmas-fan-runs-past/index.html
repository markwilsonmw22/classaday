Every year on November 1st: the Christmas fan dashes past

An eight-second skit: someone calmly grabs a drink while the household's biggest Christmas fan sprints past behind them, trailing lit-up string lights.

KlingVeoChatGPTGemini

Why it's trending

A making-of post dated November 3, 2026 says a one-take, eight-second family skit called "my wife every year on November 1st" reached 1.4 million views on TikTok in two days, and that a second couple's copy passed 1.3 million. Our earlier November 1st pack restyled a room; this one is the quick comedy skit.

How to use it

  1. Ask the person who'll star as the Christmas fan if they're happy to be in it. Use only photos of people who agreed.
  2. Film it for real in one take, or make it with AI: upload a photo of each of you as references in Kling or Veo and paste the main prompt.
  3. Use ChatGPT or Gemini to write the on-screen caption, then add it in your editor, not in the video prompt.
  4. Add a cleared holiday sound in TikTok or Reels and keep it under ten seconds.

Prompts

Main prompt (video)
Use the attached photos as the two people. Eight seconds, vertical 9:16, phone camera at chest height, in a bright, ordinary home kitchen in daylight. Shot 1 (2 seconds): the first person opens the fridge and takes out a drink, calm and a little tired. Shot 2 (3 seconds): behind them, the second person, wearing a fuzzy red reindeer onesie, sprints past from left to right with a long strand of glowing warm-white string lights trailing behind them. Shot 3 (3 seconds): the first person slowly turns to the camera with a flat, knowing look and sips the drink. Keep both faces, hair and builds the same as in the photos. Natural movement, light comedy, no text, no logos, no other people.
Caption ideas (text)
Write 10 short on-screen captions for a skit where someone in my home goes all-in on Christmas the moment Halloween ends. Start each with "my [partner / mom / roommate / dog] every year on November 1st" or a similar plain line. Keep them kind, never mean. Under 12 words each. Canadian spelling.
Variation: your pet
Use the attached photos of me and my [dog / cat]. Eight seconds, vertical 9:16, cosy living room. I sit on the couch scrolling my phone. Behind me, my pet zooms past wearing a tiny knitted holiday sweater, with a short strand of glowing string lights tangled loosely on its back. I look up at the camera, unimpressed. Keep my face and the pet's fur and markings the same as the photos. Real animal movement. No text, no logos.
Variation: small business
Use the attached photos of me and my co-worker, who agreed to be in this. Eight seconds, vertical 9:16, inside our [café / salon / shop] during the day. I'm calmly wiping the counter. Behind me, my co-worker runs past in a festive sweater and a soft Santa-style hat, carrying a box of holiday decorations with lit string lights spilling out. I turn to the camera and raise one eyebrow. Keep the room as in the photo. No text, no logos, no other people.

Tips

  • The joke is the deadpan look at the end. Keep the first person calm and the runner fast.
  • Fake string lights or battery lights are safer than a real plug-in strand if you film it yourself.
  • Say it's AI in your caption if you made it with AI, and only use people who said yes.
  • Business accounts have a smaller music library, so pick a sound that's cleared for your account.

Results vary between tools and runs. Make a few versions and keep the best. Only use photos of yourself, or of people who have agreed, and label AI content where the platform asks.
