Wedding day, cut like a movie: soft start, big finish

Turn five to eight wedding clips into a short cinematic edit that builds to one big moment. AI plans the order, the cuts and the captions.

ChatGPTGeminiClaudeCapCut

Why it's trending

A TikTok trends roundup updated October 26, 2026 lists wedding videos re-edited into romantic, cinematic montages set to a new pop song that is also on that week's trending sounds chart. Couples start with quiet moments and build to a final reveal timed to the song's closing line.

How to use it

  1. Pick five to eight short clips from your wedding day: getting ready, the first look, the aisle, a hug, the kiss, a dance.
  2. Paste a plain list of your clips into the main prompt and let AI put them in an order that builds.
  3. Pick the trending sound in the app, then line up your biggest moment with the song's final line.
  4. Keep transitions simple (straight cuts or a soft fade) and add one short caption at the start or the end.

Prompts

Main prompt
I'm making a 20 to 30 second vertical video from our wedding footage. It should start soft and quiet and build to one big emotional moment at the very end. Here are my clips, with a few words about each: [list your clips, e.g. "1. Mom doing up my dress, 2. first look by the lake, 3. walking down the aisle"]. Put them in the best order, say roughly how many seconds each one should get, tell me which clip should be the final reveal, and suggest where to slow a clip down. Then write five short captions (under 10 words) that feel romantic but not cheesy.
Variation: anniversary edit
We're celebrating [number] years together. Plan a 30-second video that starts with a few clips from our wedding day and ends with clips from our life now (our home, our kids or pets, a recent trip). Give me the clip order, where to cut from then to now, and four captions that feel warm and a little funny.
Variation: no video, just photos
I only have photos from our wedding, no video. Plan a 20-second slideshow from [number] photos that builds like a movie: which photo goes first, which goes last, how long each one stays on screen, and which ones to give a slow zoom. Add three caption ideas.
Variation: small business
I'm a [wedding photographer / videographer / florist / venue / baker]. A couple has said yes to me sharing their wedding footage. Plan a 25-second highlight edit that builds to one big moment, and write a caption that thanks the couple by first name only (if they agree) and says what I did for the day. Don't make up reviews, quotes or booking numbers.

Tips

  • Save the best moment for last. The whole format is a slow build to one payoff.
  • Fewer clips work better. Five to eight is plenty for 30 seconds.
  • Use the app's own trending sound so the music is licensed.
  • Ask before you post clips that show guests, family or kids up close.

Results vary between tools and runs. Make a few versions and keep the best. Only use photos of yourself, or of people who have agreed, and label AI content where the platform asks.
