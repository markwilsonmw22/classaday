Ads, analytics, email & conversion · Day 25 of 30

Email marketing and automation

3.5 hours

Build a permission-based email list, set up automated flows, write campaigns that get clicks, and make sure emails reach the inbox.

Lesson 40 minExample 25 minTasks 145 min≈ 3.5 h total

1Lesson

Email is the channel a business owns. No algorithm decides who sees it, and for many businesses it produces the highest return of any channel. It's also an easy upsell for an agency.

Choosing a platform

Platform Best for
Mailchimp General small businesses, easy start
Klaviyo E-commerce (deep Shopify integration, strong flows and segmentation)
MailerLite Simple, affordable, good for creators and small businesses
Brevo Email + SMS, generous sending on lower plans
Shopify Email Simple campaigns for Shopify stores

Permission first: CASL and email lists

For marketing emails (newsletters, promotions), Canada's anti-spam law requires consent. The gold standard is express consent:

  • A clear request ("Get our monthly specials by email"), saying who is asking and why.
  • An unticked checkbox or a separate signup; never pre-ticked.
  • Records of when and how each person signed up.
  • Every email includes sender identification and a working unsubscribe.

Some implied consent exists (for example, a customer who bought in the last two years), but it expires. Use double opt-in (a confirmation email) to keep lists clean and provable.

Growing the list

  • An offer worth signing up for: 10% off, a free guide, a birthday treat, early access.
  • Signup points: website popup (after a few seconds or on exit), footer, checkout, in-store QR code, booking forms, social bio link.

The automated flows

Flow Trigger Emails Example
Welcome Joins the list 2–4 over a week Welcome + offer → story → best sellers or services → reminder
Abandoned cart / browse (e-commerce) Leaves without buying 2–3 "Still thinking?" → answer objections → small incentive
Post-purchase / post-service Buys or job completed 2–3 Thank you → how to get the most from it → review request
Win-back No purchase in 60–120 days 2 "We miss you" → offer
Birthday / anniversary Date field 1 A treat
Seasonal reminder (services) Date since last service 1–2 "It's been a year since your furnace tune-up"

Campaigns

Send regular campaigns (weekly or monthly). One main message per email, a clear subject line, a preview line that adds to it, short paragraphs, one main button, and mobile-friendly design.

Segmentation

Send the right email to the right people: new vs repeat customers, location, interests, past purchases, engagement. Segmented emails get more clicks and fewer unsubscribes.

Deliverability: reaching the inbox

  • Authenticate the sending domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC records (your email platform gives you the values). Since 2024, Gmail and Yahoo require these for bulk senders, along with one-click unsubscribe and keeping spam complaints very low (under 0.3%). Do it for every client regardless of size.
  • Send from the client's domain (not a free Gmail address).
  • Clean the list: remove addresses that bounce and people who haven't opened or clicked in 6–12 months (after a re-engagement attempt).

Metrics

Open rates are unreliable since Apple's Mail Privacy Protection preloads emails. Focus on clicks, conversions, revenue per recipient, unsubscribe rate and spam complaints.

SMS

Text messages have huge open rates but need express consent under CASL too, and should be used sparingly (order updates, appointment reminders, rare high-value offers).

2Worked example

Northfield's "Never forget a tune-up" email system (Mailchimp).

  • Signup: a checkbox (unticked) on every invoice and booking form: "Send me seasonal maintenance reminders and offers from Northfield Plumbing. Unsubscribe anytime." 1,240 customers opted in over six months.
  • Flows: post-service thank-you + review request (day 1), "how to keep your drains clear" tips (day 14), and an annual reminder 11 months after a water heater flush or furnace tune-up.
  • Monthly campaign: one seasonal tip + one offer.
  • Domain authenticated (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) before the first send.

Result over a year (example numbers): the annual reminder flow alone booked 186 repeat jobs, about $34,000 in revenue, from emails that send themselves.

★Tip

For service businesses, the most profitable email is often the boring reminder: "It's been a year since...". Set it up once and it books jobs forever.
