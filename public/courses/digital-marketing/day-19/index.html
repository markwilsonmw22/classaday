Social media marketing & management · Day 19 of 30

Scheduling, community management and the tools

3.5 hours

Run social media for clients like an agency: approval workflows, scheduling tools, inbox management, moderation and a crisis plan.

Lesson 40 minExample 30 minTasks 140 min≈ 3.5 h total

1Lesson

Creating content is half the job. The other half is managing it: getting approvals, publishing on time, replying to people, protecting the brand, and doing it for several clients without chaos.

Scheduling and management tools

Tool Good for Notes
Meta Business Suite Facebook + Instagram scheduling, unified inbox, insights Free; the default for most local clients
Buffer Simple scheduling across many platforms Free plan for a few channels; clean and easy for clients
Later Visual Instagram and TikTok planning Visual grid preview; link-in-bio page
Metricool Scheduling + analytics + competitor tracking Generous free plan for one brand; good reports
Hootsuite Larger teams, many accounts Paid; strong approvals and inbox
Sprout Social Agencies with bigger clients Premium; excellent inbox, listening and reports
Agorapulse Inbox-heavy community management Paid; strong for moderation and teams

Free plans and features change often; check each tool's current plan before choosing. Start with Meta Business Suite + Buffer or Metricool, and upgrade when you have five or more clients.

Access: never ask for passwords

Get proper access instead:

  • Facebook and Instagram: the client adds your agency's business portfolio as a partner (in Meta's business settings) with access to their Page and Instagram account.
  • TikTok: TikTok Business Center lets clients grant access to members and partners.
  • LinkedIn: the client adds you as a Page admin.
  • Scheduling tools: the client connects their accounts to your workspace, or invites you to theirs.

If a platform genuinely needs a shared login, store it in a password manager (Bitwarden or 1Password) and turn on two-factor authentication.

The approval workflow

  1. You draft the month in the calendar or scheduler.
  2. The client reviews by a set date (for example, the 25th for next month), commenting directly on each post.
  3. You make changes in one round.
  4. You schedule, and they get a summary.

Agree in the contract: one revision round included, approval deadline, and what happens if they miss it ("we'll post evergreen content until approval").

Community management

Task Standard
Comments Reply within 24 hours (business days); like or reply to every genuine comment
DMs Reply within business hours; use saved replies for common questions (hours, prices, booking)
Reviews Reply within 2–3 days (Day 9)
Spam and abuse Hide or delete spam and abusive comments; use hidden words filters on Instagram and Facebook
Complaints Never delete genuine complaints. Reply publicly once with empathy, then move to DMs or phone
Escalation Anything legal, medical, safety-related or angry goes to the owner the same day

Write a community guide for each client: tone, saved replies, what you can answer, what you must escalate, and who to call.

Crisis plan

When something goes wrong (a bad viral review, a mistake, an outage):

  1. Pause all scheduled posts immediately.
  2. Alert the client and agree who speaks.
  3. Acknowledge quickly and honestly: "We've seen this, we're looking into it, we'll update you by 3pm."
  4. Fix and explain what happened and what's changing.
  5. Follow up and resume normal posting only when it's calm.

Social listening

Know what people say even when they don't tag the brand: Google Alerts (free) for the business name, platform search for the name and common misspellings, and paid tools like Brand24 or Sprout Social's listening for bigger clients.

2Worked example

Riya's client operations, one month in.

  • Meta Business Suite for Northfield's Facebook and Instagram, connected through partner access (no passwords).
  • Metricool for Corner Grind (Instagram, TikTok, Google Business Profile) with the calendar shared for approvals.
  • Approval deadline: the 25th of each month. Corner Grind's owner comments on posts from her phone in 10 minutes.
  • 14 saved replies for the café (hours, oat milk, Wi-Fi password policy, catering, gift cards, jobs).
  • Community guide: any complaint about food safety or an allergy goes to the owner by phone within the hour.

In week three, a customer posts that her drink contained dairy despite asking for oat milk. Riya pauses that day's promo post, calls the owner, and the owner replies publicly within 40 minutes with an apology, a refund and a staff training note. The customer updates her comment: "Handled really well. I'll be back."

★Tip
