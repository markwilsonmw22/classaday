"Sorry, I lied": undo the AI glow-up one slide at a time

Post an over-polished AI version of an old school or baby photo, then take back each edit, slide by slide, until the real photo shows up.

ChatGPTGemini

Why it's trending

An article published October 27, 2026 on AOL describes the viral "I'm so sorry" trend spreading beyond costumes: people post an AI-polished version of an everyday photo, like a school portrait or baby picture, then walk back the edits with captions until the unedited original is the punchline. It builds on our "I'm so sorry" costume pack with a slide-by-slide confession.

How to use it

  1. Pick an old photo of yourself, like a school picture or a baby photo. The more awkward, the better.
  2. Use the main prompt to make a polished version with three or four obvious upgrades.
  3. Use the step-back prompt to remove one upgrade at a time. Save each version.
  4. Post a slideshow: the polished one first, then each step back with a caption like "Sorry, I lied, my hair was never that shiny", and the real photo last.

Prompts

Main prompt
Using my uploaded photo, keep my face, age, expression and pose the same so it's clearly still me. Make four upgrades that are easy to spot: 1) swap the background for a dramatic, softly lit studio backdrop, 2) make my outfit look sharp and well-fitted, 3) give my hair a smooth, glossy, styled look, 4) add warm, flattering light like a professional portrait. Keep it looking like a real photo, not a cartoon. No text, logos or brand names.
Variation: step back one edit
Take the last image you made and undo only one upgrade: [the background / the outfit / the hair / the lighting]. Put that part back exactly as it was in my original photo. Keep everything else the same, with the same crop and framing, so the slides line up.
Variation: caption ideas
I'm posting a slideshow that starts with an AI-polished photo of me and takes back one edit per slide. The edits were: [list them]. Write one short, funny confession caption for each slide in the style "Sorry, I lied…", plus a final caption for the real photo. Keep it light and kind to my younger self. Under ten words each.
Variation: small business
Using my uploaded photo of our first shop sign, first product or first market table, keep the same layout and items but make a polished, magazine-style version with three clear upgrades: better lighting, tidier styling and a cleaner background. Don't add logos, prices or text. (I'll take back each upgrade slide by slide and end on the real first attempt.)

Tips

  • Always end on the real photo. Owning up is the whole joke.
  • Keep the same crop on every slide so each change is easy to see.
  • Use your own photos. Only include friends or family if they say yes first.
  • Three or four steps is plenty. More than that drags.

Results vary between tools and runs. Make a few versions and keep the best. Only use photos of yourself, or of people who have agreed, and label AI content where the platform asks.
