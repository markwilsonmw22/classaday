Foundations & your agency · Day 4 of 30

Set up your agency: brand, legal and toolkit

3.5 hours

Give your agency a name, a professional presence, the paperwork basics and a lean tool stack, so you're ready to take on clients.

Lesson 30 minExample 20 minTasks 160 min≈ 3.5 h total

1Lesson

You don't need an office or a logo designer to start. You need to look credible, be legally set up and have the right tools. All of it can be done this week for very little money.

Name and brand

A good agency name is easy to say, easy to spell, has an available .com or .ca, and doesn't box you in if you expand later. Test it by saying it on the phone: "Hi, it's Riya from Maple and Pixel."

Brand basics: a simple wordmark logo (Canva), two brand colours, one font, a professional email on your domain, and a consistent profile photo across LinkedIn, Instagram and your site.

Legal and money basics (Canada)

This is general information, not legal or tax advice. Check your province's rules or speak to an accountant.

  • Business structure: most agencies start as a sole proprietorship. In Ontario, register your business name through the Ontario Business Registry if it isn't your own legal name. Incorporate later when profit and risk grow.
  • GST/HST: you must register once your taxable sales pass $30,000 over four consecutive calendar quarters. In Ontario, you'll then charge 13% HST to Canadian clients.
  • Bank account: a separate business account and card from day one.
  • Insurance: ask a broker about professional liability (errors and omissions) and general liability insurance. Some clients will ask for proof.
  • Contracts: never start work without a signed agreement (Day 28 gives you a template).
  • Records: keep every receipt and invoice. Set aside 25–30% of profit for tax until an accountant gives you a better number.

Your agency website

One page is enough to start: who you help, what you do, proof (or "how we work" if you have no case studies yet), packages or "starting at" prices, a booking link and your contact details. The Northline or Keystone template from the SchoolAura pack works well.

LinkedIn is your shop window

Clients will look you up. Update your headline to say who you help and how: "Helping London home-service companies get more calls from Google | Local SEO & Google Ads". Post twice a week about what you're learning and doing.

2Worked example

Maple & Pixel goes live in one afternoon.

  • Domain mapleandpixel.ca bought; Google Workspace set up with riya@mapleandpixel.ca.
  • Business name registered online with the Ontario Business Registry.
  • Business chequing account opened. Wave connected for invoices.
  • Insurance: two quotes requested from brokers for errors and omissions cover.
  • Website: the Keystone template recoloured to deep green and cream, with three packages and a Calendly button. Published on Vercel the same day.
  • HubSpot Free CRM with a pipeline: Researching → Contacted → Call booked → Proposal sent → Won / Lost.
  • LinkedIn headline rewritten; first post: "I'm starting a marketing agency for London trades. Here's the first thing I noticed reading 120 plumber reviews..."

Total spend so far: domain, one month of Workspace and a name registration fee.
