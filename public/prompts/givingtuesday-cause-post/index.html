GivingTuesday post: share the cause you care about, in your own words

Write a short, honest GivingTuesday post and a matching graphic for a cause you support, or for your shop's give-back day on December 1.

ChatGPTGeminiClaude

Why it's trending

GivingTuesday falls on Tuesday, December 1, 2026. CanadaHelps runs a GivingTuesday 2026 hub with resources for charities, and Canadian charities are already setting up GivingTuesday 2026 campaign pages. We didn't find view counts for a specific AI trend, so this is a seasonal pack, not a viral one.

How to use it

  1. Pick one cause or charity and find its real website or donation page.
  2. Paste the post prompt and fill in why it matters to you. Use only facts from the charity itself.
  3. Paste the graphic prompt into an image tool to make a simple square image to go with it.
  4. Check every name, date and link, then post on December 1.

Prompts

Post prompt: why I give
Help me write a short GivingTuesday post for Instagram and Facebook, for Tuesday, December 1. The cause is [name of the charity or cause]. It matters to me because [one or two sentences in your own words]. Write it in a warm, plain voice, under 90 words. Start with a short personal line, then say what the charity does in one sentence, then ask friends to give, volunteer or share if they can. No guilt, no pressure, no made-up numbers or facts. End with a spot for the link: [link].
Graphic prompt: a simple square image
Make a square social media graphic for GivingTuesday on December 1. Soft, warm style: two hands holding a small glowing paper heart, on a cream background with a few hand-drawn snowflakes. Warm red and soft gold colours. Large, clean text at the top: "GivingTuesday · December 1". Small text at the bottom: "[cause name]". Spell every word correctly. No other text, no logos.
Variation: give without money
I want to take part in GivingTuesday on December 1 but can't donate this year. Give me 10 ways to give back in my Canadian town that cost little or nothing, like volunteering, donating items or helping a neighbour. Keep each idea to one short sentence. Then write a 50-word post inviting friends to pick one and join me.
Variation for a small business: a give-back day
I run a small bookshop in Canada. On GivingTuesday, December 1, we are [describe what you are really doing, for example: collecting new books for a local literacy group]. Write a short, honest post announcing it, under 80 words. Say clearly what we're doing, who it helps, and how people can join in. No fake urgency, no exaggerated claims, no made-up totals. Add a friendly line thanking customers.

Tips

  • Link straight to the charity's own page so people know the money goes to the right place.
  • Chatbots can get facts about charities wrong. Copy any facts from the charity's website, not from the AI.
  • If you run a business give-back, say exactly what you'll give and only promise what you'll really do.
  • Short and personal works better than a long list of facts.

Results vary between tools and runs. Make a few versions and keep the best. Only use photos of yourself, or of people who have agreed, and label AI content where the platform asks.
