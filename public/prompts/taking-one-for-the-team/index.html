Taking one for the team

A short bit about the role you always end up with: the driver, the photographer, the planner. AI helps you write the setup and the punchline.

ChatGPTGeminiClaude

Why it's trending

An Instagram Reels trends roundup updated October 23, 2026 lists "Taking one for the team" as a trend from October 16: a role-based bit about the person who always takes the job nobody wants, like the designated driver or the group photographer.

How to use it

  1. Pick your role: the driver, the photographer, the one who books the table, the one who carries everything.
  2. Use the main prompt to get on-screen text ideas and pick your favourite.
  3. Film a short clip of you in that role, like holding everyone's bags or taking the group photo.
  4. Add the text over the clip and post with a trending sound from the app.

Prompts

Main prompt
I'm always the [your role, e.g. designated driver / group photographer / planner] in my friend group. Write 8 short on-screen text ideas for a "taking one for the team" video about it. Each one has a setup line and a punchline, both under 12 words. Use real details: [two or three things that always happen to you in this role]. Make it fond and funny, not bitter. No hashtags, no emojis.
Variation: Halloween night
Write 6 "taking one for the team" on-screen texts about the person who does the boring job on Halloween night: walking the kids around, handing out candy at the door, or wearing the costume nobody else wanted. My role is [your role]. Setup and punchline, each under 12 words. Warm and silly.
Variation: small business
I run [your type of business]. Write 6 "taking one for the team" on-screen texts about the jobs someone on my team always takes on, like [examples: closing up, cleaning the espresso machine, answering the phone during the rush]. Setup and punchline, each under 12 words. Make the team look good and human. Don't make up numbers, reviews or offers.

Tips

  • The more specific the detail, the funnier it is. "Holding six coats at the door" beats "helping out".
  • Show the role on screen so people get the joke before they read it.
  • Tag friends only if they're happy to be in it.
  • If you're the driver, keep filming to when you're parked.

Results vary between tools and runs. Make a few versions and keep the best. Only use photos of yourself, or of people who have agreed, and label AI content where the platform asks.
