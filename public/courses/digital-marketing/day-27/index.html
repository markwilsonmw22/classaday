Run and grow the agency · Day 27 of 30

Finding clients

3.5 hours

Build a repeatable system for finding clients: warm network, the mini-audit method, compliant cold outreach, LinkedIn, local networking and referrals.

Lesson 45 minExample 30 minTasks 135 min≈ 3.5 h total

1Lesson

Every agency's biggest problem in year one is the same: not enough clients. The fix isn't luck, it's a daily outreach system you run whether you feel like it or not.

The pipeline maths

Work backwards from your goal:

Clients needed ÷ close rate = sales calls needed Sales calls ÷ reply-to-call rate = replies needed Replies ÷ reply rate = personal outreaches needed

Example: 2 new clients a month, a 40% close rate → 5 calls → if half of replies book a call, 10 replies → at a 15% reply rate for well-researched personal outreach, about 65–70 outreaches a month, or 3–4 a day.

Channel 1: your warm network

Tell 30 people what you do, specifically: "I help home-service companies in London get more calls from Google." Ask: "Who do you know who'd want that?" Your first clients very often come from here.

Channel 2: the mini-audit (Loom) method

The most effective cold outreach for agencies: give value first.

  1. Pick a high-scoring prospect from your Day 14 list.
  2. Spend 15 minutes finding 3 specific problems and 1 quick win (from Search Console-style checks, their Google Business Profile, ads, reviews, site speed).
  3. Record a 3-minute Loom walking through them, showing their own site.
  4. Email it with a short, personal message.

A cold email that respects CASL

Subject: 3 quick fixes for Northfield's Google listing

Hi Dave,

I was looking for an emergency plumber in Byron last night and noticed three things that are probably costing you calls: your Google profile is missing the "Water heater installation" category, 14 of your service pages are hidden from Google, and your best reviews aren't on your website.

I recorded a 3-minute video showing exactly what I found and how to fix it, free to use whether we work together or not: [Loom link]

If it's useful, I'm happy to walk you through it on a 15-minute call.

Riya Shah · Maple & Pixel · 100 Example St, London ON · mapleandpixel.ca Not relevant? Reply "no thanks" or [unsubscribe] and I won't email again.

It's relevant to his role, uses his published business email, identifies the sender and includes an unsubscribe (Day 14).

Follow-up cadence

Most replies come after a follow-up. A polite sequence: Day 0 email → Day 3 short follow-up ("did the video come through?") → Day 7 a different value point → Day 14 a friendly close ("I'll stop here, the video's yours to keep"). Then stop.

Channel 3: LinkedIn

  • Optimised profile (Day 4) and two posts a week about your niche.
  • Connect with owners in your niche with a short, personal note. Engage with their posts before pitching.
  • Message only after a real interaction; offer the mini-audit.
  • Don't use automation tools that break LinkedIn's terms; accounts get restricted.

Channel 4: local and partnerships

  • Chamber of commerce, BIA events, BNI or similar referral groups, trade association meetups.
  • Referral partners who serve the same clients but don't compete: web designers, accountants, printers, sign makers, business coaches. Offer a referral fee or a reciprocal arrangement.

Channel 5: content and inbound

Post useful content for your niche (case studies, quick tips, before/after results). It's slower, but it makes outreach warmer: prospects check your profile and see proof.

Run it daily

Block 60–90 minutes every morning: research 3 prospects, record 3 Looms, send emails, do follow-ups, comment on 5 LinkedIn posts. Track everything in your CRM pipeline.

2Worked example

Maple & Pixel's first 30 days of outreach.

  • Warm network: 30 messages → 2 intro calls → 1 client (Corner Grind, through a friend).
  • Mini-audit emails: 40 Looms to high-scoring home-service businesses → 9 replies → 5 calls → 2 clients (Northfield and an HVAC company).
  • LinkedIn: 2 posts a week; 60 personalised connections; 3 conversations; 1 proposal pending.
  • Chamber of commerce mixer: met a web designer who now refers clients who need marketing after a new website.

Time: about 75 minutes a day. Three new retainers in month one, worth $3,650 a month combined.

★Tip

Personalise the first line with something only that business would recognise: a review, a photo, a page. Generic emails get deleted; specific ones get replies.
