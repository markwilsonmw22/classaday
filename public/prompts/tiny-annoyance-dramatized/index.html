Tiny everyday annoyance, played like a tragedy

Act out one small, relatable annoyance in a short loop, with way too much drama. AI finds the annoyances and writes the captions.

ChatGPTGeminiClaude

Why it's trending

A UGC trend report covering October 17 to 24, 2026 names relatable micro-problems as a top format this week. Its example is an eight-second loop that dramatizes water running down your arm while washing your face, with no product in sight.

How to use it

  1. Use the main prompt to get a list of tiny annoyances almost everyone has lived through.
  2. Pick one you can act out at home in under 10 seconds.
  3. Film it with big, serious drama, then add the caption on screen.
  4. Trim it so the end flows back into the start, so it plays as a loop.

Prompts

Main prompt
I'm making 8-second videos where I act out a tiny everyday annoyance like it's a tragedy. Write 15 relatable micro-problems that almost everyone has had, that I can film at home with no special props. Example: "Water running down your arm while you wash your face." For each, add a short on-screen caption under 10 words and one note on how to act it out. Harmless and funny. No real people, no brands, no health, money or politics, no emojis.
Variation: Canadian fall and winter
Write 10 tiny annoyances that come with fall and early winter in Canada, like a wet glove, a foggy windshield, or a toque that keeps sliding up. Each one must be something I can act out in under 10 seconds. Give a short caption under 10 words for each. Playful, no emojis.
Variation: small business
I run [your type of business]. Write 10 tiny, relatable annoyances from my line of work that I can act out in a short, dramatic loop. Example for a florist: "The one stem that won't stand up straight." Give each a caption under 10 words. Light and self-aware, never blaming a customer. No emojis.

Tips

  • The smaller the problem, the bigger the reaction should be.
  • Keep it under 10 seconds. Short loops get watched twice.
  • If you sell something, leave it out of the first clip. Let the joke land first.
  • Read the AI's list and cut anything that could hurt someone's feelings.

Results vary between tools and runs. Make a few versions and keep the best. Only use photos of yourself, or of people who have agreed, and label AI content where the platform asks.
