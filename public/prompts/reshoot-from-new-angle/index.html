Wrong angle? Reshoot your photo in 3D

Turn one good photo into a 3D scene, then walk the camera around it to get the overhead, low or close-up shot you missed.

ChatGPTHiggsfield

Why it's trending

On October 19, Higgsfield added 3D Angles to its ChatGPT plugin. It turns a photo, a video or a written description into a 3D scene you can move through and reshoot, with lens, aperture and frame size controls, as a photo or a short video.

How to use it

  1. Pick a sharp, well-lit photo where the whole subject is visible (a dish, a product, a room, your pet on the couch).
  2. Open the Higgsfield plugin in ChatGPT, choose 3D Angles and upload the photo.
  3. Move the camera to the angle you want, then paste one of the prompts below to set the lens and the look.
  4. Save the new shot, or ask for a short video of the camera move.

Prompts

Main prompt
Rebuild my photo as a 3D scene and keep every object, colour and detail exactly as it is. Reshoot it from directly above, looking straight down, like a flat-lay. Use a 35 mm lens, sharp focus from edge to edge, and soft, even daylight with gentle shadows. Square 1:1 frame. Do not add or remove anything.
Variation: low and dramatic
Rebuild my photo as a 3D scene and keep the subject exactly the same. Move the camera down to just above the floor, looking up at the subject so it feels big and important. Use a 24 mm wide lens, keep the background softly blurred, and add a little warm light from one side. Tall 9:16 frame.
Variation: slow orbit video
Rebuild my photo as a 3D scene with nothing added or changed. Make a 6-second video where the camera circles slowly halfway around the subject at eye level, then stops on a clean side view. Use an 85 mm lens with a softly blurred background. Smooth, steady movement, natural light. 9:16.
Variation: small business product set
Rebuild my product photo as a 3D scene and keep the product exactly as it is, including the label text and colours. Give me four new shots for my online shop: straight on, a three-quarter view from the left, a close-up of the best detail with a 100 mm lens at f/2.8, and a top-down view. Clean, bright, even light and the same background in every shot. Square 1:1.

Tips

  • The more of the subject your photo shows, the better the hidden sides will look. A photo taken from a slight angle helps.
  • Small moves look the most real. Swinging all the way to the back means the AI has to guess what it never saw.
  • Longer lenses (85 mm and up) flatter faces and products. Wide lenses (24 mm) feel bold and dramatic.
  • For products you sell, always check the new shots against the real item so the photos stay honest.

Results vary between tools and runs. Make a few versions and keep the best. Only use photos of yourself, or of people who have agreed, and label AI content where the platform asks.
