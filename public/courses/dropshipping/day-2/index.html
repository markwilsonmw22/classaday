Foundations · Day 2 of 30

The numbers: margin, break-even and ad cost per sale

3.5 hours

Calculate the profit on one order and the most you can pay in ads to get a sale without losing money.

Lesson 45 minExample 30 minTasks 135 min≈ 3.5 h total

1Lesson

Every decision in this business comes back to one question: how much can I spend to win a customer and still make money? Today you learn to answer it in two minutes for any product.

The parts of one sale

For each order you pay four kinds of cost:

  • Product cost (COGS): what the supplier charges for the item.
  • Shipping: what the supplier charges to deliver it.
  • Payment fees: usually around 2.9% + 30¢ per order with card processors. Check your plan's exact rate.
  • Ad cost per sale (CPA): total ad spend divided by number of sales.

Three numbers to know by heart

1. Gross margin before ads

Price − product − shipping − fees

In the figure: $34.99 − $8.40 − $4.20 − $1.31 = $21.08.

2. Break-even CPA

This is the same number. If one sale costs you $21.08 in ads, you make exactly $0. Spend less per sale and you profit. Spend more and you lose.

3. Break-even ROAS

ROAS means return on ad spend: revenue divided by ad spend. Ad platforms report it, so you need to know your break-even:

Break-even ROAS = Price ÷ Gross margin

$34.99 ÷ $21.08 = 1.66. If Meta shows a ROAS above 1.66, you're profitable on that product (before fixed costs like apps).

Why price matters so much

Look at what happens if you sell the same product at $24.99 instead:

$24.99 price $34.99 price
Product + shipping $12.60 $12.60
Fees $1.02 $1.31
Gross margin $11.37 $21.08
Break-even ROAS 2.20 1.66

The cheaper price almost halves the money you can spend to get a customer. Since ad costs are similar either way, low-priced products are much harder to make profitable. A good rule: your selling price should be at least 2.5–3× your product-plus-shipping cost, and ideally over $25.

Average order value (AOV)

If some customers buy two, your average order goes up while ad cost per order stays about the same. That's why bundles and "buy 2, save 15%" offers (Day 18) are so powerful.

Fixed costs

Apps, your Shopify plan and your domain are fixed monthly costs. Add them up and divide by expected monthly orders to see what each order must also cover. Early on, keep fixed costs under $100 a month.

Try it yourself

Put your own product's numbers in. The calculator uses the same formulas as above.

2Worked example

Priya runs the numbers on a self-cleaning slicker brush.

  • Supplier price: $6.90, shipping to Canada and the US: $4.10. Total landed cost: $11.00
  • Planned price: $32.99
  • Fees: 2.9% × 32.99 + 0.30 = $1.26
  • Gross margin: 32.99 − 11.00 − 1.26 = $20.73
  • Break-even CPA: $20.73
  • Break-even ROAS: 32.99 ÷ 20.73 = 1.59

She also tests a two-pack at $54.99 (landed cost $19.20): margin = 54.99 − 19.20 − 1.89 = $33.90. If one in four buyers takes the two-pack, her average margin per order rises from $20.73 to about $24.02, so she can afford more per sale in ads. She adds both lines to a Calculator tab in her sheet.

★Tip
