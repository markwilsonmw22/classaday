# Before you bake

Good cakes come from a few habits more than from secret recipes. Read this chapter once, and every recipe in this book will work better.

## Weigh, don't scoop

Every recipe gives grams first, with cups as a backup. A cup of flour can weigh anywhere from 120 g to 160 g depending on how you fill it, which is the difference between a tender cake and a dry one. A digital kitchen scale costs less than a cake tin and removes the guesswork.

If you must use cups, **spoon the flour into the cup and level it with a knife**. Never dip the cup into the bag.

## Room temperature matters

Butter, eggs and milk should sit out for about an hour before baking, so they blend into a smooth batter that traps air.

- **Butter** is ready when your finger leaves a dent but doesn't sink in (about 18–20°C / 65–68°F). Too soft and the cake will be dense and greasy.
- **Eggs:** place cold eggs in a bowl of warm tap water for 10 minutes.
- **Milk or buttermilk:** microwave in 10-second bursts until no longer cold.

## Know your oven

Most home ovens run 10–25°F (5–15°C) hotter or cooler than the dial says. An **oven thermometer** placed in the middle of the oven tells you the truth. Always preheat for at least 20 minutes, and bake on the middle rack.

## Prepare your pans

Grease the pan, line the base with a circle of parchment, grease the parchment, then dust with flour (or cocoa for chocolate cakes). Your layers will release cleanly every time.

**Bake-even strips** (wet fabric strips wrapped around the pan) help layers bake flat with less doming, so there's less to level off later.

## Mixing methods

- **Creaming method** (butter cakes): beat butter and sugar for 3–4 minutes until pale and fluffy, add eggs one at a time, then alternate dry ingredients and liquid. Gives a light, classic crumb.
- **Oil method** (chocolate, carrot, red velvet): whisk wet ingredients, then stir into dry. Gives a moist cake that stays soft in the fridge.
- **Reverse creaming:** mix butter into the dry ingredients first, then add liquids. Gives a fine, velvety crumb that's very stable for stacking.

Once flour is added, **mix only until you can't see dry flour**. Over-mixing makes cakes tough and tunnelled.

## How to tell when it's done

- The top springs back when pressed lightly.
- The edges pull away from the sides of the pan.
- A toothpick in the centre comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs (not wet batter).

Cool layers in the pan for 10 minutes, then turn out onto a rack. Cool completely before frosting. **For decorating, chill layers wrapped in plastic for at least 2 hours, or overnight.** Cold cakes are much easier to level, stack and frost.

## Changing pan sizes

Use the pan's area to scale a recipe. The two-layer 8-inch recipes in this book fill:

| Pan | Approx. area | Batter vs. two 8-inch rounds | Bake time (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two 8-inch rounds | 100 sq in | 1× (as written) | 28–35 min |
| Two 9-inch rounds | 127 sq in | 1.25× | 25–32 min |
| Three 6-inch rounds | 85 sq in | 0.85× | 22–28 min |
| 9 × 13 inch rectangle | 117 sq in | 1.15× | 30–40 min |
| 24 cupcakes | — | 1× | 18–22 min |

Thinner layers bake faster. Always check 5 minutes before the shortest time.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix next time |
|---|---|---|
| Domed, cracked top | Oven too hot, too much batter | Check oven temperature; use bake-even strips |
| Sank in the middle | Underbaked, oven door opened early, too much leavening | Bake until it springs back; don't open the door for the first 20 min |
| Dry and crumbly | Too much flour, overbaked | Weigh flour; check early; brush layers with syrup |
| Dense and heavy | Butter too soft or cold, under-creamed | Room-temperature ingredients; cream for a full 3–4 min |
| Stuck to the pan | Pan not lined | Grease, parchment, grease, flour |
| Tunnels and holes | Over-mixed after adding flour | Mix just until combined |

# The cake bases

Seven reliable cakes, all sturdy enough for stacking and decorating. Each makes two 8-inch layers unless noted. Bake them ahead: wrapped well, cooled layers keep in the fridge for 3 days or the freezer for 2 months.

::: recipe
title: Classic Vanilla Butter Cake
tag: Cake base
intro: The everyday birthday cake. A tender, buttery crumb that's sturdy enough to stack and decorate.
meta: Makes two 8-inch layers | Serves 12–14 | Prep 25 min | Bake 28–32 min | 350°F / 175°C
ingredients:
- 315 g (2½ cups) all-purpose flour
- 2½ tsp baking powder
- ½ tsp fine salt
- 226 g (1 cup) unsalted butter, room temperature
- 350 g (1¾ cups) granulated sugar
- 4 large eggs, room temperature
- 1 tbsp pure vanilla extract
- 240 ml (1 cup) whole milk, room temperature
method:
1. Heat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease and line two 8-inch round pans.
2. Whisk the flour, baking powder and salt in a bowl.
3. Beat the butter and sugar on medium-high speed for 3–4 minutes until pale and fluffy. Scrape the bowl.
4. Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each. Beat in the vanilla.
5. On low speed, add the flour mixture in three parts, alternating with the milk in two parts, starting and ending with flour. Mix only until no dry flour remains.
6. Divide evenly between the pans (weigh them for equal layers) and smooth the tops.
7. Bake for 28–32 minutes, until golden and a toothpick comes out clean.
8. Cool in the pans for 10 minutes, then turn out onto a rack to cool completely.
notes:
- For a whiter cake, use 6 egg whites instead of 4 whole eggs, and clear vanilla.
- Brush cooled layers with Simple Syrup (see Frostings & fillings) to keep them moist under fondant.
:::

title: Eggless Vanilla Sponge
tag: Cake base
intro: A soft, light vanilla cake made without eggs, using yogurt for lift and moisture. Perfect for egg-free celebrations.
meta: Makes one 8-inch layer (double for two) | Serves 8 | Prep 15 min | Bake 30–35 min | 350°F / 175°C
ingredients:
- 240 g (1 cup) plain yogurt, room temperature
- 150 g (¾ cup) granulated sugar
- ½ tsp baking soda
- 120 ml (½ cup) neutral oil
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- 190 g (1½ cups) all-purpose flour
- 1¼ tsp baking powder
- ¼ tsp fine salt
- 80–120 ml (⅓–½ cup) milk, as needed
method:

::: recipe
title: Black Forest Cake
tag: Cake assembly
intro: Chocolate sponge, cherries and whipped cream, finished with chocolate shavings. A celebration classic.
meta: Serves 14 | Prep 45 min plus chilling | Uses the chocolate fudge cake
ingredients:
- 1 Rich Chocolate Fudge Cake, two 8-inch layers, chilled
- 1 jar (680 g / 24 oz) pitted sour cherries in syrup
- 2 tbsp cherry liqueur or kirsch (optional)
- 1 batch Stabilised Whipped Cream, doubled
- 100 g dark chocolate, for shavings
- 12 fresh or maraschino cherries with stems, to decorate
method:
1. Drain the cherries, keeping the syrup. Mix 80 ml (⅓ cup) of syrup with the liqueur, if using.
2. Level both cake layers and slice each in half horizontally to make four thin layers.
3. Place one layer on a cake board. Brush generously with cherry syrup.
4. Spread with a 1 cm layer of whipped cream and scatter a third of the cherries over it.

# Frostings & fillings

The right frosting depends on the job: smooth sides, sharp piping, a light filling or a glossy drip. Each recipe below says what it's best for.

| Frosting | Texture | Best for | Holds in warm weather |
|---|---|---|---|
| American buttercream | Sweet, dense | Beginners, piping, kids' cakes | Good |
| Swiss meringue buttercream | Silky, less sweet | Smooth finishes, elegant piping | Fair |
| Cream cheese frosting | Tangy, soft | Carrot and red velvet, rustic finishes | Poor, keep chilled |
| Chocolate ganache | Glossy or fudgy | Drips, glazes, under fondant | Good |
| Stabilised whipped cream | Light, airy | Black Forest, fruit cakes | Poor, keep chilled |

::: recipe
title: American Buttercream
tag: Frosting
intro: Quick, sweet and sturdy. The easiest frosting to learn piping with.
meta: Makes about 4 cups | Fills and coats one 8-inch layer cake | Prep 10 min
ingredients:
- 226 g (1 cup) unsalted butter, softened
- 450 g (about 3¾ cups) icing sugar, sifted
- 3–4 tbsp whipping cream or milk
- 2 tsp vanilla extract
- ⅛ tsp fine salt
method:
1. Beat the butter on medium-high for 3 minutes until very pale.
2. Add the icing sugar a cup at a time on low speed.
3. Add the vanilla, salt and 3 tbsp cream. Beat on medium-high for 3–4 minutes until light and fluffy.
4. Adjust: add more cream for softer frosting, or more icing sugar for stiffer piping.
5. For smooth sides, stir by hand with a spatula for 1–2 minutes to press out air bubbles.
notes:
- Double the recipe if you plan to pipe borders and decorations as well.
- Stiff consistency for flowers and borders; medium for coating; soft for writing.
:::

::: recipe
title: Swiss Meringue Buttercream
tag: Frosting
intro: Silky, glossy and less sweet. The professional choice for smooth sides and sharp edges.
meta: Makes about 5 cups | Prep 30 min | Needs a stand mixer and thermometer
ingredients:
- 150 g egg whites (about 5 large eggs)
- 250 g (1¼ cups) granulated sugar
- 340 g (1½ cups) unsalted butter, cubed, soft but cool
- 2 tsp vanilla extract
- ⅛ tsp fine salt
method:
1. Wipe the mixer bowl and whisk with a little vinegar to remove any grease.
2. Whisk the egg whites and sugar in the bowl over a pan of gently simmering water until the mixture reaches 71°C (160°F) and feels completely smooth between your fingers.
3. Move the bowl to the mixer and whip on high for 8–10 minutes until thick, glossy and the bowl feels cool.
4. Switch to the paddle. On medium speed, add the butter a few cubes at a time.
5. Keep beating. It may look curdled or soupy; this is normal. It will come together into a smooth buttercream within a few minutes.
6. Beat in the vanilla and salt.
notes:
- Soupy and won't come together? Chill the bowl for 10 minutes, then beat again.
- Curdled and lumpy? It's too cold. Warm the outside of the bowl briefly with a hair dryer while beating.
- Keeps 1 week in the fridge. Bring to room temperature and re-beat before using.
:::

::: recipe
title: Chocolate Ganache, Four Ways
tag: Frosting
intro: Just chocolate and cream. Change the ratio and you change the job it does.
meta: Prep 10 min | Ratios are chocolate to cream, by weight
ingredients:
## Choose your ratio
- **Drip (dark):** 100 g dark chocolate : 100 g cream (1:1)
- **Drip (milk):** 200 g milk chocolate : 100 g cream (2:1)
- **Drip (white):** 300 g white chocolate : 100 g cream (3:1)
- **Frosting or under fondant (dark):** 200 g dark chocolate : 100 g cream (2:1)
- **Truffles:** 2:1, chilled until firm
method:
1. Chop the chocolate finely and place in a heatproof bowl.
2. Heat the cream until it just begins to steam and bubble at the edges.
3. Pour the cream over the chocolate and leave for 3 minutes without stirring.
4. Stir from the centre outwards until smooth and glossy.
5. For drips, cool to about 30–32°C (86–90°F): it should feel just slightly warm. For frosting, leave at room temperature until it's the texture of peanut butter, 1–3 hours.
notes:
- Test a drip on the side of a cold glass before doing the cake.
- Too thick? Warm in 5-second bursts. Too thin? Wait a few more minutes.

title: Stabilised Whipped Cream
tag: Filling
intro: Light whipped cream that holds its shape for days in the fridge instead of weeping after a few hours.
meta: Makes about 4 cups | Prep 15 min
ingredients:
- 480 ml (2 cups) cold whipping cream, 35%
- 30 g (¼ cup) icing sugar
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
## Stabiliser, choose one
- 1 tsp unflavoured powdered gelatin + 4 tsp cold water

# Decorating techniques

This chapter takes you from four plain cake layers to a smooth, professional-looking cake, then through the piping and finishing techniques used in the design projects.

{{svg:cake_layers|A cross-section of a properly built layer cake.}}

## Step 1: Level and tort

1. Chill the layers for at least 2 hours so they don't crumble.
2. Set a layer on the turntable. Hold a serrated knife level with the lowest point of the dome.
3. Turn the cake slowly while sawing gently, letting the turntable do the work, until the dome comes off.
4. To **tort** (split a layer in two), score a line all the way round at half the height, then cut along it.

## Step 2: Fill and stack

1. Put a dab of frosting on the cake board and press the first layer on, cut side up.
2. Brush with syrup if using.
3. For soft fillings (curd, jam, fruit, whipped cream), **pipe a ring of stiff buttercream around the edge** first. This "dam" stops the filling squeezing out.
4. Spread the filling inside the dam, 6–10 mm thick.
5. Add the next layer and repeat. Place the final layer **cut side down**, so the top is perfectly flat.
6. Look at the cake from eye level and press gently to straighten it.

## Step 3: Crumb coat

Spread a thin layer of frosting over the top and sides, just enough to trap loose crumbs and fill gaps. It doesn't need to look neat. **Chill for 20–30 minutes** until firm to the touch.

## Step 4: The final coat

1. Pile a generous amount of frosting on top and spread it to the edges with an offset spatula, letting it overhang.
2. Cover the sides thickly using the spatula.
3. Hold the bench scraper **upright against the side at a 45° angle**, touching the board. Spin the turntable with your other hand in one smooth turn. Scrape excess back into the bowl.
4. Fill any gaps with more frosting and scrape again. Repeat until smooth.
5. A ridge of frosting will rise above the top edge. Chill for 10 minutes, then sweep it inwards with a clean offset spatula from the edge to the centre for a **sharp edge**.
6. For an extra-smooth finish, dip the scraper in hot water, dry it, and do one last pass.

## Piping basics

- **Fill the bag** only two-thirds full, twist the top closed and hold the twist between thumb and forefinger. Squeeze from the top with your whole hand, not your fingertips.
- **Angle:** 90° (straight up) for rosettes, stars and dots; 45° for shells, borders and writing.
- **Pressure:** steady pressure for even shapes. To finish, stop squeezing, then pull away.
- **Practise** on parchment or an upside-down plate. Scrape it back into the bag and go again.

## Six piping designs

| Design | Tip | How |
|---|---|---|
| Rosette | Open star 1M | Start in the centre, squeeze and circle outwards once, release and flick away |
| Star | 1M or 2D | Hold at 90° just above the surface, squeeze, stop, lift |
| Shell border | 1M or 21 | At 45°, squeeze to build a mound, ease off while pulling forward to a tail; start the next shell on the tail |
| Drop flower | Closed star 2D | At 90°, squeeze while turning your wrist a quarter turn, stop and lift; add a dot in the centre |
| Ruffle | Petal #104 | Wide end touching the cake, move the bag up and down in a tight zigzag as you travel |
| Writing | Round #2–#3, soft frosting | Touch, lift slightly, squeeze evenly, let the line fall into place; touch down to finish |

## Colouring buttercream

- Use **gel colours** and add them with a toothpick; colours deepen over 1–2 hours, so mix lighter than you want.
- For deep colours (red, black), start from chocolate buttercream for black, and make red the day before.
- For **ombré**, divide frosting into 3–4 bowls and add increasing amounts of the same colour.

## Drips

1. Make drip ganache (chapter 3) and cool to 30–32°C (86–90°F).
2. **The cake must be cold**, straight from the fridge.
3. Put the ganache in a squeeze bottle or piping bag with a small cut. Test one drip at the back.
4. Squeeze gently at the top edge and let each drip run down. Vary the length by changing the pressure.
5. Fill in the top with the remaining ganache and smooth it with an offset spatula.

## Naked and semi-naked

A **naked cake** shows the layers on the outside: fill and stack, then scrape the sides with only a whisper of frosting. **Semi-naked**: apply a thin coat and scrape most of it off so the cake shows through in places. Brush layers with syrup first; naked cakes dry out faster. Serve within a day.

## Covering a cake with fondant

1. Give the cake a firm final coat of buttercream or 2:1 ganache and chill until firm.
2. Knead the fondant until soft and pliable. Use roughly **500 g for a 6-inch cake, 750 g for an 8-inch, and 1 kg for a 9–10-inch** cake that is 4 inches tall.
3. Dust the work surface lightly with cornflour and roll the fondant to about 4 mm thick, in a circle wider than the cake's diameter plus twice its height.
4. Lift it over your rolling pin and drape it over the cake.
5. Smooth the top first with a fondant smoother, then work down the sides, lifting and flaring the skirt to remove pleats. Never stretch it downwards.
6. Trim the base with a sharp knife or pizza cutter and polish with the smoother.

## Stacking tiers

{{svg:tiers|How a two-tier cake is supported. The dowels, not the cake, carry the weight of the top tier.}}

1. Put each tier on its own cake board the same size as the cake.
2. Press a dowel into the bottom tier, mark it level with the top, pull it out and cut 4–6 dowels to that height.
3. Insert them in a circle slightly smaller than the top tier's board.
4. Spread a little frosting where the top tier will sit, then centre the top tier on its board.
5. For transport, push a sharpened central dowel down through both tiers.

Approximate guide. A 6-inch on an 8-inch two-tier cake serves about 32 party slices.

## Storing and transporting

- Buttercream cakes keep at cool room temperature for a day, or 3–4 days in the fridge. Bring to room temperature for 1–2 hours before serving for the best flavour and texture.
- Cream cheese and whipped cream cakes must stay in the fridge.
- Transport in a box on a flat surface (car floor or boot, not on a seat), chilled, with non-slip matting underneath.

# Design projects

Four cakes that put every technique together. Each lists what to bake, what to make and the order to work in.

::: recipe
title: Ombré Rosette Cake
tag: Design project · Beginner
intro: Rows of piped rosettes fading from deep to pale. Looks impressive and hides an imperfect finish.
meta: Serves 14 | 2 hours decorating | Tip: open star 1M
ingredients:
- 1 Classic Vanilla Butter Cake, two 8-inch layers, chilled
- 2 batches American Buttercream
- Pink or any gel colour
- 4 piping bags, 1 open star tip 1M
method:
1. Level the layers, tort them into four and stack with buttercream. Crumb coat and chill.
2. Spread a thin, smooth white coat over the cake and chill for 15 minutes.
3. Divide the remaining buttercream into four bowls. Leave one white and colour the others light, medium and dark.
4. Fill four bags with the tip moved between them, or use four tips.
5. Starting at the base with the darkest colour, pipe a row of rosettes around the cake, each touching the last.
6. Pipe the next row above with medium, then light, then white rosettes covering the top edge and spiralling to the centre.
7. Chill for 30 minutes to set before moving or slicing.
notes:
- Work on a turntable and pipe each row in one go so the spacing stays even.
:::

::: recipe
title: Birthday Drip Cake
tag: Design project · Intermediate
intro: Smooth sides, a chocolate drip and a crown of sweets and rosettes. The modern birthday classic.
meta: Serves 16 | 3 hours decorating | Tips: 1M, round #12
ingredients:
- 1 Rich Chocolate Fudge Cake, two 8-inch layers, chilled
- 1 batch Swiss Meringue Buttercream (or 2 batches American)
- 1 batch dark drip ganache (1:1)
- Gel colour of your choice
- Toppings: chocolate bars, macarons, sprinkles, a cake topper
method:
1. Level, tort and stack the layers with buttercream. Crumb coat and chill for 30 minutes.
2. Colour most of the remaining buttercream. Apply the final coat, scraping smooth with a sharp top edge. Chill for at least 1 hour.
3. Press sprinkles onto the bottom 2 cm of the sides, if using.
4. Cool the ganache to 30–32°C and test one drip at the back. Apply drips all round, then fill the top.
5. Chill for 15 minutes to set the drip.
6. Pipe 8–10 rosettes in a crescent on one side of the top. Arrange chocolates, macarons and the topper in and around them.
notes:
- Arrange toppings in odd numbers and keep them to one side of the top for a modern look.
:::

::: recipe
title: Semi-Naked Floral Cake
tag: Design project · Beginner
intro: Rustic, elegant and quick. Ideal for showers, small weddings and anyone who doesn't love heavy frosting.
meta: Serves 14 | 1.5 hours decorating | No piping needed
ingredients:
- 1 Lemon Yogurt Drizzle batter baked as two 8-inch layers, or Classic Vanilla
- 1 batch Swiss Meringue Buttercream or Cream Cheese Frosting
- 1 jar lemon curd or 250 g fresh berries
- Fresh edible flowers or herbs, washed and dried; berries to garnish
- Small pieces of floral tape and cling film for any non-edible stems
method:
1. Brush layers with lemon syrup. Stack with a buttercream dam and a layer of curd or berries inside each.
2. Apply a thin coat all over.
3. Holding the scraper at 45°, scrape off most of the frosting so the cake shows through, leaving a frosted haze. Chill.
4. Wrap the stems of any flowers in floral tape and cling film so they never touch the cake directly.
5. Arrange flowers and berries in a cluster sweeping down one side and across the top.
notes:
- Only use flowers sold as edible or grown without sprays. Many florist flowers are treated with chemicals and some are toxic.
:::

::: recipe
title: Two-Tier Celebration Cake
tag: Design project · Advanced
intro: A 6-inch tier on an 8-inch tier, smooth white finish and a simple piped detail. The plan for your first tiered cake.
meta: Serves about 32 | Plan over 3 days | Tips: round #3, 2D
ingredients:
- 1 batch Classic Vanilla Butter Cake (two 8-inch layers)
- 1 batch Classic Vanilla Butter Cake baked in three 6-inch pans (use 2 layers, enjoy the third)
- 2 batches Swiss Meringue Buttercream
- One 8-inch and one 6-inch cake board, plus a 10-inch drum
- 5 dowels or wide bubble-tea straws
- Fresh flowers or sugar flowers to finish
method:
1. **Day 1:** bake all layers, cool, wrap and chill. Make the buttercream and refrigerate.
2. **Day 2:** bring the buttercream to room temperature and re-beat. Level, fill and crumb coat both tiers on their own boards. Chill.
3. **Day 2:** apply final coats with sharp edges. Chill overnight.
4. **Day 3:** fix the 8-inch tier to the drum with buttercream. Insert dowels cut to its exact height (see Stacking tiers).
5. Lift the 6-inch tier with an offset spatula under the board and centre it on top.
6. Pipe a small shell or bead border round the base of each tier to hide the joins.
7. Add the flowers in a cascade from the top tier down one side. Keep chilled until 1–2 hours before serving.
notes:
- Write a timeline and stick to it. Rushing the chilling steps is the main cause of leaning tiers.
:::

# Planning a celebration cake

## A simple timeline

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 week before | Confirm size, flavour, design and dietary needs. Order or buy toppers and tools |
| 3 days before | Shop for ingredients. Make syrup and any sugar decorations |
| 2 days before | Bake layers, cool, wrap and chill. Make frosting |
| 1 day before | Level, fill, crumb coat, final coat. Chill overnight |
| The day | Drips, piping, flowers and toppings. Box and transport chilled |

## Flavour pairings that work

| Cake | Filling | Frosting |
|---|---|---|
| Vanilla | Raspberry jam | Vanilla Swiss meringue |
| Chocolate | Salted caramel | Chocolate ganache |
| Red velvet | Cream cheese | Cream cheese |
| Carrot | Toasted pecans | Cream cheese with cinnamon |
| Lemon | Lemon curd | Vanilla buttercream |
| Eggless vanilla | Mango purée | Whipped cream |
| Chocolate | Cherries | Whipped cream (Black Forest) |

> **Allergy note:** always ask about allergies and label celebration cakes for guests: wheat, eggs, milk, tree nuts and peanuts are common in decorated cakes, and many sprinkles and decorations contain several allergens.
