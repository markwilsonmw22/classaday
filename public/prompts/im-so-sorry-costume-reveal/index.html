"I'm so sorry" costume glow-up reveal

Post a glammed-up AI version of an old Halloween costume photo, then confess with the real, homemade original on the last slide.

ChatGPTGemini

Why it's trending

A Know Your Meme guide published October 22, 2026 explains the "I'm so sorry" slideshow trend: creators open on a flattering, edited photo, then reveal the real one with an apology. It says the trend surged in October, mostly with old Halloween costumes.

How to use it

  1. Find an old Halloween photo of yourself in a homemade or not-so-great costume.
  2. Upload it with the main prompt to make a polished, over-the-top version.
  3. Make a slideshow: the glow-up first, one or two close-ups of the "best" details, then the real photo last.
  4. Put "I'm so sorry" on the final slide. The honest reveal is the joke.

Prompts

Main prompt
Using my uploaded photo, keep my face, expression, pose and body exactly the same. Upgrade my homemade Halloween costume into a polished, film-quality version of the same idea: rich fabrics, neat seams, detailed props and proper makeup. Put me in a moody, well-lit Halloween setting with soft fog and warm practical lights. Make it look like a real high-end photo, not a cartoon. Don't add any logos, brand names or text.
Variation: detail close-ups
From the upgraded costume photo, make two close-up crops that show off the most impressive details, like the props, fabric texture or makeup. Keep everything identical to the full image so the slides match.
Variation: small business
Using my uploaded photo of our shop's first-ever Halloween display, keep the same layout and products but show it as a polished, magazine-quality version with better lighting, tidy styling and a cosy spooky mood. Don't add logos, prices or any text. (I'll post the real first attempt on the last slide.)

Tips

  • Always end on the real photo. The trend only works because you own up.
  • The worse the original costume, the better the payoff.
  • Only use photos of friends or family if they say yes first.
  • Keep the slides in the same crop so the reveal feels like a before and after.

Results vary between tools and runs. Make a few versions and keep the best. Only use photos of yourself, or of people who have agreed, and label AI content where the platform asks.
