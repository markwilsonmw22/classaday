I moved from here to there: 7 things nobody told me

Turn your own move, city to city or province to province, into a numbered list of oddly specific local discoveries. AI helps you dig them out and polish them.

ChatGPTGeminiClaude

Why it's trending

A weekly UGC trend report for October 17 to 24, 2026 names "lived-specificity advice hooks" as a format working this week: numbered, very local observations from someone who moved, with moving from the American South to Minnesota as its example.

How to use it

  1. Use the main prompt and answer its questions about your move. The details have to come from you.
  2. Pick the 5 to 7 most specific, surprising points. Cut anything that could be said about any city.
  3. Film one short clip or take one photo for each point, or talk to the camera with the list on screen.
  4. Open with the hook line from the prompt and end by asking locals what you missed.

Prompts

Main prompt
I moved from [where you lived before] to [where you live now] about [how long] ago. Interview me with 10 short questions to pull out small, specific things that surprised me: weather, food, how people talk, driving, prices of everyday things, unwritten rules. Ask one question at a time. When I'm done, turn my answers into a numbered list of 7 points, each under 20 words, plus 3 hook lines like "I moved from X to Y. Here's what nobody told me." Only use what I told you. Don't invent facts about either place.
Variation: Canadian moves
I moved within Canada, from [city or province] to [city or province]. Help me brainstorm categories of small differences I might have noticed: winter habits, words people use, coffee and food, transit, how people greet strangers, sports, store hours. Ask me about each one, then write a numbered list from my answers only. Keep it friendly. Poke fun at myself, not at the people who live there.
Variation: new to a job or school
Use the same format, but for starting a new job, school or hobby group instead of a new city: "I started [new thing] after [old thing]. 7 things nobody told me." Ask me questions first, then write the list from my answers. Keep it kind and don't name the company, school or any real person.
Variation: small business
I run a [type of business] in [town or neighbourhood]. Write a list post: "7 things you only know if you live in [neighbourhood]", based on these notes from me: [paste your notes]. Work in one natural mention of my business, not a sales pitch. Only use facts from my notes.

Tips

  • Specific beats general. "People leave their cars running in the parking lot in February" works. "The weather is different" doesn't.
  • AI doesn't know your town. Let it ask the questions and shape the list, but every fact should come from you.
  • Laugh at yourself, not at the locals. The comments stay friendlier.
  • Don't show your street, house number or anything that pins down where you live.

Results vary between tools and runs. Make a few versions and keep the best. Only use photos of yourself, or of people who have agreed, and label AI content where the platform asks.
